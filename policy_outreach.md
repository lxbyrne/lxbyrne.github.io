# Policy and Outreach

{:.lead}

I am passionate about the intersection of science and policy, whether it using scientific research to support policymaking or advocating for policies that support scientific research. I have used my skills as a scientist to advance efforts in both of these areas through a number of science policy organizations. I have also been active in my communities through social, professional, and educational outreach initiatives.

## MIT External Affairs Board

As a member of the MIT External Affairs Board, I helped advocate for graduate students at the local, state, and federal levels of government. In March of 2025, I traveled to Capitol Hill to speak with congressional offices about federal science funding, protections for international students, and bridge programs that help students from underrepresented backgrounds access graduate education.

Following this trip, I was elected as a Public Outreach and Communications Chair for the External Affairs Board. In this role, I established collaborations with the MIT Writing Center, MIT Communication Lab, Knight Science Journalists, and ComSciCon to support new advocacy initiatives at MIT. In particular, I helped lay the groundwork for science policy op-ed writing and high school outreach programs. I also acted as a liaison between the External Affairs Board and other science policy organizations to expand our advocacy efforts.

## Committee on the Future of Graduate Education

I volunteered as part of a small group of five graduate students to examine ways in which Congress could help improve graduate programs. We identified multiple challenges that graduate students across the country face such as imbalanced power dynamics, lack of professional development support, and variable mentorship quality. My contributions particularly focused on the professional development side, where I found that increasingly more PhD graduates are leaving academia while academic training continues to neglect the skills needed to succeed outside of academia. 

We concluded that many of these structural problems relate to the research-based funding model that funds graduate students but only incentivizes research progress. We thus proposed the creation of more fellowships and trainee grants in order to support the holistic development of the future STEM workforce. I helped author a 22-page policy document that clearly outlined these issues and our proposed solutions. I also helped present this report to science-based nonprofits and congressional offices in a briefing on Capitol Hill. 

## Scientist Network for Advancing Policy

I am also a member of the Scientist Network for Advancing Policy (SNAP), a grassroots organization of early-career researchers who are passionate about connecting science to policymakers and community members. I helped organize congressional visits to advocate for science funding in the summer of 2025, one of SNAP's first large-scale initiatives. Over the course of only a few weeks, we trained 144 early-career researchers and enabled 54 congressional visits across 29 states. I led the mobilization of scientists in Maryland and Florida where I helped participants prepare for meetings and develop talking points, created one-pagers using state-specific examples of federal science funding, and coordinated with congressional staffers to schedule meetings.

Outside of congressional visits, I am active in a number of areas related to community building, organizational development, and communication. I have helped onboard new members and continue to participate in "inreaching" efforts to build a stronger and more inclusive community. In June of 2026, I took part in the first ever SNAP retreat where we discussed the future of SNAP as an organization. I co-led a session that examined the role of SNAP in the science policy ecosystem and explored areas for future expansion and collaboration. Furthermore, I am involved in writing and editing for the SNAP blog as well as the development of a new SNAP podcast.

## Courses, Workshops, and Trainings

Science intersects with society in many ways, but it is never the only only consideration when it comes to shaping policy. I have taken classes to learn how scientific knowledge must be balanced with political, social, and economic factors. One of these was a science and technology policy bootcamp taught by William Bonvillian, former director of the MIT Washington Office. Over the course of a week, we discussed topics including the economic drivers between science and technology innovation, the organization of federal science support, and the science policy landscapes of the life science, energy, and manufacturing sectors. Inspired by the discussion on challenges in emerging renewable energy technologies, I wrote about policy pathways for marine carbon dioxide removal for my final project. I also audited a class on environmental science and policy taught by acclaimed climate scientist Susan Solomon. This course covered the science, history, and current regulations surrounding issues such as lead, smog, and climate change.

Additionally, I have participated in a number of trainings and workshops such as the three-day Fundamentals of Climate Science and Policy symposium at MIT, the 2026 American Association for the Advancement of Science National Meeting, and an American Chemical Society webinar on speaking with congressional offices.

## Science Communication

I believe that scientific impact begins with thoughtful communication. As a scientist who frequently interacts with people from many different disciplines, I have learned how to identify my audience, use clear and concise language, and help them see why the science is worth caring about. I am experienced in communicating with scientists, policymakers, and the general public in both written and verbal formats. 

I also believe that communication is essential for increasing accessibility in science. I wrote about these topics in a [piece for the MIT grad blog](https://oge.mit.edu/weak-signals-can-build-strong-bonds/) and a co-authored [piece for the SNAP blog](https://medium.com/science-policy-in-a-snap/what-does-it-mean-to-be-a-scientist-and-who-gets-to-decide-158f971112b0). As a teaching assistant and peer tutor, I have communicated complex scientific concepts to many students and helped them become confident in their scientific identities. I have also interviewed five researchers for Astrochem Coffee, a podcast designed to provide news on recent research in astrochemistry to scientists across a range of career stages.

## Community-Building and Mentorship

During my time as a graduate student, I have worked to bring improve wellbeing and belonging by creating welcoming communities. As a member of the Chemistry Graduate Student Committee, I planned department-wide social and professional development events. I represented this group on the MIT Chemistry Quality of Life committee where I worked to improve graduate student and postdoc experiences through programs such as research and mentorship awards.

I am very grateful to have had amazing mentors throughout my research career. I care deeply about paying this forward by helping my own mentees navigate the "hidden curriculum" of graduate education and research. I completed a certificate in research mentoring and applied these skills to mentor two undergraduate students. As a peer mentor, I helped first-year graduate students adjust to campus life, choose a research advisor, and balance teaching with their own coursework. I also helped prospective students from underrepresented backgrounds apply for graduate admission through the Chemistry Application Mentorship Program.
